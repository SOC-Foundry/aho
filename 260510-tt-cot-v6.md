## Statement of Intent

Tachtech runs on three commitments to our customers: **their data is theirs, our code is honest, and the path between the two is auditable end-to-end.** This document is how we keep those commitments. It is not aspirational. It is not theoretical. **This is the architecture we operate.**

We do not build a perimeter and trust everything inside it. We do not bolt agents onto a flat network and call ourselves zero-trust. We build a **chain** in which every link is enforced by a different vendor, evaluated against a different signal, and logged to a different audit stream. **No single product compromise breaks the fortress.** No single stolen credential reaches a customer's data. No single misconfigured policy collapses the perimeter.

We hold this standard because our customers do regulated, consequential work. They came to Tachtech because they did not trust their data with general-purpose vendors. We do not give them a reason to question that decision.

Ten layers. Eleven enforcement planes. **One trust decision, evaluated everywhere.** Every layer fails closed. Every layer logs to a single SIEM. Every layer is owned by a different boundary.

## L0 — Perimeter: Ubiquiti UDM-SE

### What we defend against
The internet probes our WAN every minute of every day. Most of it is automated, most of it is dumb, and **all of it deserves to be dropped before it touches any other layer.** Volt Typhoon lived in SOHO routers for years using ordinary-looking outbound connections. Mirai turned consumer perimeter devices into a 1Tbps cannon. The first thing an attacker sees of Tachtech is the DMP — and the DMP is built to lie convincingly.

### What we deploy
- **IPS mode (drop, not alert).** Threat Management runs at "Security" sensitivity with versioned suppression list in our security repo. We drop on Critical, High, and Medium signatures. Auto-update Suricata rules daily.
- **GeoIP filtering.** Countries with no Tachtech business are blocked outbound and inbound. Tor exit nodes are blocked. This is noise-floor reduction — every alert that reaches our SIEM is from a population we cannot dismiss out of hand.
- **DNS Shield.** The DMP intercepts and inspects DNS at the perimeter even when an endpoint is configured with a rogue resolver. This is the third line of DNS defense, behind Cloudflare Gateway (L1) and the device's own resolver.
- **Application-aware firewall.** We block by application class, not just port. RDP, VNC, AnyDesk, TeamViewer, BitTorrent, Tor, cryptocurrency protocols — dropped at the edge regardless of port.
- **Honeypot.** A fake `172.30.254.99` listens on common service ports inside our lab VLANs. **Any traffic to it is, by definition, malicious.** A non-zero hit on the honeypot is an active intrusion and pages on-call.
- **Backup VPN.** The DMP's native WireGuard server is configured as our break-glass remote access — independent of Tailscale's control plane. If Tailscale ever has a control-plane outage, we still reach the network. This is the third independent path that exists precisely so we never need it.
- **Locked management plane.** Zero WAN-side admin access. UI reachable only over LAN — and after L3 ships, only over Tailscale. Local admin password rotated quarterly, stored in `vault-lab-keys`. Cloud SSO via Okta.

### Why this is non-negotiable
The DMP is the only control that operates **before identity is even evaluated.** Every other layer assumes "an attacker has a session." The DMP rejects them while they're still anonymous. When a customer asks us "what stops a botnet from probing your services?" — the answer is the DMP, and it has been doing its job since the moment we plugged in the WAN cable.

## L1 — DNS + Egress: Cloudflare Zero Trust Gateway

### What we defend against
**Outbound is where data dies.** Every breach of consequence in the last decade ends in an exfiltration step. Inbound walls catch the headline attacks; outbound policy catches the actual damage. Beyond exfiltration, every modern malware family relies on outbound DNS for command and control — and 90% of phishing domains are less than seven days old. We do not let any of this reach our endpoints unchallenged.

### What we deploy
- **Global DNS resolver.** Cloudflare Gateway is the resolver for every Tachtech device — including internal lookups. Every DNS query from our infrastructure is inspected against threat intelligence before it returns an answer. No internal MagicDNS bypass; we route `.ts.net` and `.lab.local` via explicit split-DNS rules so we get the inspection plane *and* the convenience.
- **WARP client on every endpoint.** "Gateway with WARP" mode, DNS locked to Gateway, always-on. A user on hotel Wi-Fi has the same DNS protection as a user at our office.
- **Block at DNS layer:** malware C2, phishing, DGA, DNS tunneling, newly-registered domains under 30 days, cryptomining, P2P, anonymizers, DoH/DoT to non-Cloudflare resolvers.
- **Block at HTTP layer:** uploads to non-corporate cloud storage, downloads from non-allowlisted domains without Safe Browsing pass, paste-bin classes of services.
- **Remote Browser Isolation** for unknown URLs from email links — the page renders in Cloudflare's browser, never on the laptop.
- **Inline DLP** scans every egress for AHO API keys, AWS credentials, Stripe keys, GitHub PATs, and our project keyword dictionaries. Hits block at the wire and alert in the SIEM.
- **CASB** monitors connected SaaS (Notion, GitHub, Workspace) for misconfigurations: public links, external collaborators, weak admin MFA. Drift is alerted within minutes.
- **Cloudflare Tunnel** is how we expose anything publicly. No port forwarding on the DMP, ever. Outbound-only TLS to Cloudflare's edge, Cloudflare Access (Okta-gated) on top.
- **On-prem fallback resolver.** Unbound runs on our subnet routers as a last-resort DNS path. If Cloudflare ever has a regional incident, our network stays operational on degraded security policy. The runbook says: if fallback is engaged for more than two hours, declare a security event.

### Why this is non-negotiable
Without L1, exfiltration is a forensics exercise. With L1, exfiltration is a policy decision **logged at the moment it was attempted, blocked at the wire, and alerted to a human**. The day a customer asks "did our data leave?" we answer in seconds, with a query, with confidence — not in days, with a guess.

## L2 — Identity: Okta + 1Password + Google Advanced Protection

### What we defend against
Identity is the most-attacked surface in modern environments. The 2022 Uber breach was a stolen contractor credential. The 2023 Okta support-system compromise was a stolen admin token. The 2022 LastPass breach was server-side and the offline-cracked vaults still hurt users years later. Every identity choice we make assumes the attacker is competent and assumes they will eventually attempt every form of credential theft, social engineering, and session replay we have not specifically defended against.

### What we deploy

**Okta as the singular identity plane.**
Okta is our only IdP. Every SaaS we use is federated to Okta — no exceptions, no local accounts, no shared logins. Email/password fallback is disabled tenant-wide on every connected app once SSO is verified. SCIM is enabled wherever the SaaS supports it: Tailscale, Notion, GitHub, Cloudflare, Claude Enterprise, AWS, GCP, 1Password. **When we offboard a user from Okta, every downstream system loses them within minutes — automatically, auditable, no human in the loop.**

**Phishing-resistant authentication everywhere it is supported.**
Primary factor for every Tachtech engineer is a passkey stored in 1Password. Passkeys are FIDO2 under the hood — they bind the assertion to the requesting domain, which means the entire class of real-time relay phishing attacks (Evilginx, EvilProxy) **simply does not apply to us.** TOTP exists only as a documented fallback for the dwindling set of legacy systems that do not yet support passkeys, and we treat that list as a risk register entry to be eliminated, not a default.

**1Password as the credential plane.**
Every credential in our org lives in 1Password — or it does not exist. Vaults are SCIM-provisioned by Okta group membership. The 1Password tenant itself is hardened with account-level FIDO2 hardware keys (YubiKey 5C NFC, two per administrator), 128-bit Secret Keys printed on paper and stored in the safe, and Travel Mode enforced for any laptop crossing a customs border. The 2022 LastPass scenario — server-side vault dump followed by offline cracking — does not apply to us: the Secret Key is the key our threat model assumes the attacker does not have.

Every engineer's daily workflow uses `op run` for secret injection. Plaintext secrets do not exist on disk anywhere in our infrastructure. CI/CD runners use 1Password Service Accounts with read-only, vault-scoped, audited tokens — every secret read is logged, every anomaly is alerted.

**Google Advanced Protection Program for the Engineering OU.**
Every engineer holds two YubiKeys — primary on a keyring, backup in their safe — enrolled to Google APP. APP forces hardware-key MFA on the Workspace identity, blocks the third-party-app-access vector that bit Mailchimp and Microsoft, and hardens account recovery against the social-engineering chains that compromise consumer-tier accounts. The Workspace identity backstops Chrome, Drive, and Gmail — three of the highest-leverage data surfaces we operate. We harden it accordingly.

### Why this is non-negotiable
A SaaS-native engineering shop without phishing-resistant MFA, SCIM-driven lifecycle, and a hardened credential plane is a breach waiting for a calendar date. We have all three. **An attacker who phishes one of our engineers gets nothing — the credential is a passkey, the passkey is bound to the domain, and the credential vault is gated by hardware keys we physically possess.** That is the security story we tell customers, and it is technically true at every layer.

## L2.5 — AI: Claude Enterprise (Triple Lock) + Sovereign On-Prem Inference

### What we defend against
AI is the fastest-growing data egress surface in 2026. The Samsung-leak pattern — engineers pasting proprietary code or client data into a public AI tool, with that data potentially absorbed into training corpora — is the cautionary tale every CISO references. Beyond Samsung, there is a class of customer engagements (regulated finance, healthcare, defense) for whom *no* third-party AI is acceptable, regardless of any vendor's no-train guarantee. We meet both threat models.

### What we deploy

**Claude Enterprise as the sanctioned AI surface.**
Anthropic's Enterprise tier gives us no-train guarantee in writing, SAML SSO via Okta, SCIM lifecycle, audit log API, and admin-controlled retention. We are deployed; this is operational. Claude is the only sanctioned AI in our environment — Chrome CBCM blocks every other AI domain by default. There is no shadow-AI surface in Tachtech.

**The Triple Lock — three orthogonal access controls.**
A Claude session at Tachtech requires *all three* of the following, evaluated independently:

1. **Identity.** Active Okta session with passkey factor — no TOTP fallback for this app.
2. **Network.** Source IP within our Tailscale tailnet or App Connector egress range. Anthropic's IP allow-list enforces this; an engineer on a personal device on a coffee-shop Wi-Fi cannot reach Claude regardless of how good their credentials are.
3. **Posture.** Active CBCM-managed Chrome profile signed in to a `tachtech.com` Workspace account, on a device with Kolide-healthy + Falcon-ZTA-≥-80 status.

Any single failure denies access. The pattern is contextual access control by design — three orthogonal signals, each enforced at a different plane, each logged independently.

**Claude Code CLI is SSO-only.**
The CLI authenticates through the same SAML flow as the web app. We do not issue raw `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` values to engineers — API keys are reserved for service accounts via 1Password Service Account injection, scoped per use case, rotated quarterly. Every CLI invocation rides the same audit pipeline as web usage.

**Prompt DLP and audit ingestion.**
Cloudflare Gateway and Chrome CBCM both scan outbound traffic to `*.anthropic.com` for AHO data signatures and client identifiers. The Claude audit log is pulled into our SIEM nightly. We sample ten random prompts per quarter and verify none contain client identifiers — that sample is our standing audit artifact for "we govern AI usage."

**Sovereign on-prem inference for client-classified engagements.**
For engagements where the customer contract excludes third-party AI inference at any tier, we operate our own. Open-weights models (Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek, Mistral) run on dedicated GPU hardware behind our tailnet, fronted by a Tailscale-bound, Okta-SAML-gated, Triple-Lock-enforced inference API. Engineers see the same `claude` CLI; the gateway routes by engagement classification — invisible to the user, enforced by policy. **This is a contract-winning capability**, not a hypothetical. Customers in regulated sectors choose Tachtech specifically because we can deliver this.

### Why this is non-negotiable
An auditor's question — "how do you govern AI usage?" — has, for us, a single-page answer: three orthogonal locks, two audit log streams, one quarterly review, and a sovereign fallback for engagements that demand it. **AI is not a side door at Tachtech. It is a governed surface.** That posture is what allows us to adopt AI aggressively in the rest of our work — because we have actually solved the governance problem rather than wished it away.

## L3 — Transport: Tailscale Mesh, /26 Segmentation, A8 as Universal Subnet Router

### What we defend against
A flat network is one breach away from total compromise. Wide subnets give attackers a wide universe to pivot through. We size segments to purpose: 62 hosts per subnet — the size of a *single function*, not a *campus*. When something goes wrong on one segment, the attacker's lateral universe is small enough to contain in minutes.

### What we deploy

**Class B address space.** `172.30.0.0/16` and `172.31.0.0/16` — chosen to avoid every common collision (`192.168/16` for home routers, `10/8` for corporate VPCs and cloud VPCs, `100.64/10` for Tailscale CGNAT). When an engineer joins a customer VPN, our routes do not collide with theirs.

**Seven purpose-built /26 segments.**

| CIDR | VLAN | Name | Purpose | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `172.31.255.128/26` | 110 | KT Lab | Kyle's primary dev (`a8cos`, `nzxtcos`) | `tag:lab-kt` |
| `172.31.255.64/26` | 111 | DL Lab | Dan's primary dev / testing | `tag:lab-dl` |
| `172.31.255.0/26` | 112 | Eng 3 Lab | Expansion / contractor | `tag:lab-eng3` |
| `172.30.254.192/26` | 120 | Eng 4 Lab | Expansion / contractor | `tag:lab-eng4` |
| `172.30.254.128/26` | 130 | POC 1 | Customer POC staging | `tag:poc-1` |
| `172.30.254.64/26` | 140 | VPC 1 | AWS/GCP connector gateway | `tag:vpc-connector` |
| `172.30.254.0/26` | 150 | Workloads | K8s, APIs, AHO engine | `tag:workload-prod` |

Within each /26: `.1` is the gateway, `.2–.20` are static reservations, `.50–.62` is the DHCP pool. DHCP Guarding is enabled on every VLAN, trusted to the UDM only — a rogue Docker DHCP server cannot poison our network.

**Inter-VLAN ACL matrix — default deny.**

| From ↓ To → | KT | DL | Eng3 | Eng4 | POC1 | VPC1 | Workloads |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **KT** | — | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| **DL** | ⛔ | — | ⛔ | ⛔ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| **Eng3** | ⛔ | ⛔ | — | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ |
| **Eng4** | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ | — | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ |
| **POC1** | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ | — | ⛔ | ⛔ |
| **VPC1** | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ | — | ✅ |
| **Workloads** | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ | ⛔ | ✅ | — |

A compromised contractor laptop on Eng4 cannot reach Workloads. A compromised host on POC1 cannot reach the VPC connector. The matrix above is the literal answer to "how do you contain a breach." It fits on one page; the auditor recognizes it; we move on.

**A8 as universal subnet router (HA secondary on `nzxtcos`).**
A8 carries trunked 802.1Q with all seven VLAN sub-interfaces configured via `systemd-networkd`. Tailscale advertises all seven /26s from this single node. ACL `autoApprovers` covers every route — no manual approval click after a reboot. A secondary subnet router on `nzxtcos` carries the same trunk and advertises the same routes; Tailscale's native HA fails over within seconds.

**Tag-based ACLs, posture-conditional.**
Every node in our tailnet is tagged. Lab nodes are `tag:lab-node`, routers are `tag:lab-router`, contractor sandboxes are `tag:contractor`, AI inference is `tag:ai-sovereign`. Tagged nodes do not have key expiry — our pipelines do not break at 3 AM on a Sunday because someone forgot to re-auth. Access is granted by `srcPosture` rules that consume both Kolide and CrowdStrike ZTA signals — an engineer on an unhealthy device is denied at the network layer regardless of their identity.

**Tailscale SSH replaces every authorized_keys file.**
Sessions authenticate against the same Okta-anchored Tailscale identity. The OS never sees a key. Every SSH session is logged in the Tailscale admin console with user, source device, posture, and timestamp. The system access log *is* the audit log.

**App Connectors lock down our admin planes.**
Okta, CrowdStrike, Cloudflare, and Anthropic admin consoles are not reachable on the public internet from any IP that is not ours. The App Connector on `a8cos` advertises those domains into the tailnet; consoles that don't speak App Connector are gated by IP allow-list to our static-egress addresses. An attacker with stolen admin credentials, signing in from any source IP not on our allowlist, gets a 403 *before* the credential is validated. They do not even get to learn whether the credential works.

### Why this is non-negotiable
Tailscale is the spine of every other layer. Identity (L2) is enforced at the network because Tailscale rides Okta SSO. Posture (L4) gates network access because Tailscale consumes Kolide and Falcon ZTA. Workload isolation (L5) is real because containers bind to `tailscale0`. **The mesh is what makes the rest of the chain a chain — not a list of products in the same Slack channel.**

## L4 — Endpoint: Kolide + CrowdStrike Falcon (Partner Tier)

### What we defend against
Identity tells us who. It does not tell us whether the laptop they hold has full-disk encryption, an unpatched kernel, a malicious browser extension, or active ransomware. The 2023 Okta support breach started with malware on a contractor's personal device that was authenticated to a privileged session. Userland security tools are the first thing modern malware disables — Lockbit, BlackByte, every kernel-rootkit family targets userland AV before payload execution. We assume our endpoints will be attacked competently and we deploy accordingly.

### What we deploy

**Kolide for compliance posture.**
Kolide answers *"is the device configured the way we said?"* — it is proactive compliance, user-mediated, friendly. Custom checks include: disk encryption (LUKS on Linux, FileVault on macOS), Secure Boot status, BBR tuning, firewall up, kernel age within 30 days, Tailscale running, no NOPASSWD sudo, 1Password CLI healthy, WARP connected and locked to Gateway, DNS resolver matches Gateway IP.

A failing check drops the user out of the trust boundary until they remediate. Kolide notifies the user directly via Slack with what to fix; the engineer fixes their own device. There is no ticket queue, no security-team intervention for routine drift.

**CrowdStrike Falcon for behavioral EDR — partner tier.**
Falcon answers *"is the device behaving the way we said?"* — it is reactive detection, kernel-level eBPF, behavioral IOA, real-time response. We deploy as **CrowdStrike partners**, which gives us privileged access to the platform and keeps our deployment posture aligned with current best practice. Our fleet is overwhelmingly Linux and macOS; the 2024 channel-file incident that affected Windows fleets globally has no relevance to our threat model.

The full Falcon stack is deployed: prevention, EDR, identity threat protection, browser sensor, container sensor, cloud workload protection. Sensor updates roll through canary → production → critical rings as standard partner-tier practice. RTR sessions are recorded; exclusions are documented per workload with date and approver. Falcon Identity Protection publishes ZTA scores back to Okta continuously — when Falcon detects ransomware behavior on an endpoint, Okta force-revokes every session for that user across every connected SaaS within seconds. **Three-second containment, four-system audit trail. This is the closed loop.**

**Why we run both.**
Kolide and Falcon are not redundant. They observe orthogonal aspects of "device trust." A malicious browser extension siphoning session cookies is invisible to Kolide; Falcon catches the syscall pattern. An unencrypted laptop with a clean Falcon score is still SOC 2 CC6.1 non-compliant; Kolide catches the missing FileVault. **One without the other is half a story.** Together they are what device trust looks like in 2026.

### Why this is non-negotiable
Without endpoint posture, network identity is a fiction. We can prove, at any moment, the configuration and behavioral state of every device on our tailnet. When we issue a session token, we know — not assume — that the receiving device is in a known-good state. When we revoke that token, we revoke it because the device told the truth about itself and the truth changed.

## L4.5 — Browser: Chrome Browser Cloud Management

### What we defend against
A modern engineer spends most of their day inside a browser. Slack web, Notion, Cloudflare dashboard, GitHub, AWS console, Claude — all browser-resident. The browser is, functionally, the new operating system. The 2024 cyberhaven extension hijack — where a single malicious extension auto-update silently exfiltrated session tokens from thousands of users — is the canonical example of why this layer cannot be left ungoverned. An unmanaged browser running an unaudited extension is the equivalent of a development laptop with no agent installed.

### What we deploy

**CBCM enrollment via Kolide push.** Every Tachtech-issued laptop carries the Chrome Enterprise enrollment token. Every Chrome instance reports to CBCM and applies policy — desktop, mobile, anywhere Chrome runs.

**Managed profile enforcement.** Sign-in is restricted to `tachtech.com`. An engineer cannot add a personal Gmail account to a Tachtech-managed Chrome profile. If they attempt to sign into a personal account, Chrome forces a separate profile with isolated cookies, storage, and extensions. The "I'll just paste this into my personal Drive" failure mode is structurally impossible.

**Extension allow-list — seven entries, total.** 1Password, Tailscale, Cloudflare WARP, Claude, uBlock Origin, our TOTP fallback authenticator, and the Kolide browser hooks. Every other extension is blocked by default. A new addition requires security-team review and a CBCM policy change — not a click-through accept.

**In-browser DLP.** Uploads, downloads, and cross-origin pastes are scanned against the same DLP signature catalog as our network egress. If an engineer copies a Drive document and pastes it into a personal Gmail draft, the paste is blocked at the browser layer before it ever reaches the network layer.

**Falcon browser sensor integration.** Catches the cyberhaven-class attack — a malicious extension auto-update behaving anomalously *post-deploy*, even when it passed allow-list review at install time. Anomalous extension behavior alerts in the SIEM and triggers CBCM remote-disable.

### Why this is non-negotiable
The browser is the last unaudited surface in any modern engineering environment. We have closed it. **An engineer cannot accidentally exfiltrate client data through the browser at Tachtech.** They cannot install a malicious extension. They cannot paste sensitive content cross-origin without an audit trail. The browser is now a governed surface, not a free-for-all.

## L5 — Workload: AHO Containers with Hardware-Attested Confidentiality

### What we defend against
A container that can reach the host network can reach everywhere the host can reach. A host operator with root can read process memory directly. The 2023 Kinsing campaign specifically targeted Docker hosts with default networking, mining crypto and pivoting laterally. For our Tier-1 customer engagements — the ones that pay the highest margins because they have the highest security bars — *encryption at rest and in transit are not enough*. We must encrypt **in use**.

### What we deploy

**Filesystem and isolation baseline.** AHO data lives on XFS with `noatime,nodiratime,inode64,logbufs=8,logbsize=256k` and quota enforcement so a runaway container cannot eat the host disk. K3s orchestrates with NetworkPolicy-as-code. Every AHO compose service binds exclusively to `tailscale0` — no `network_mode: host`, no published host ports, no container ever has a path to `192.168.x.x` or `172.16.x.x` from inside the workload.

**Runtime hardening, every container.** Read-only root filesystem, all capabilities dropped (`cap_drop: [ALL]`), `no-new-privileges`, non-root UID inside the container, image pinned by SHA256 digest — never by tag. `latest` is mutable and supply-chain-attackable; we do not run mutable references in production.

**Secrets injection at start, not at rest.** Every AHO container starts via `op run --env-file=...` — secrets materialize inside the child process's environment from 1Password Connect, scoped read-only to the workload's vault, audited per-read. The plaintext secret never lands on disk anywhere in the deployment pipeline. If A8 is stolen, the attacker holds a token good for one vault, with revocation auditable.

**Hardware attestation for Tier-1 workloads.** Customer engagements that require it run on AMD SEV-SNP. The full attestation chain: UEFI Secure Boot → measured boot via TPM PCRs → dm-verity-protected root → SEV-SNP attestation report → Tachtech attestation service compares against expected measurements → 1Password Connect releases secrets *only* if attestation passes. **An AHO container only receives its decryption keys after the silicon has cryptographically proven the workload is unmodified, from CPU up.** If anything in the stack is tampered — bootloader, kernel, image, runtime — attestation fails, secrets are not released, the workload does not start.

We use the Confidential Containers (CoCo) project on K3s for orchestration. Workload tier is a label on the deployment; the orchestrator places Tier-1 work on attested nodes automatically.

### Why this is non-negotiable
Confidential computing is the answer to *"what stops the host operator from reading our customers' data?"* Without it, the answer is administrative. With it, **the silicon won't decrypt it for them.** That is a categorical upgrade in the trust story for any engagement where insider threat is in scope, and it is a contract-winning capability for the regulated-industry customers that drive our highest-margin work.

## L6 — Knowledge & Customer Portal: Notion + Cloudflare Tunnel

### What we defend against
Internal SOPs and customer deliverables aggregate institutional knowledge that took years to build. They are the most exfiltration-attractive assets we have. The risk is mundane: a public Notion link gets indexed by search engines, or an offboarded contributor still has guest access. The control we want is structural — the access list reflects current authorized users *at this moment*, every access leaves a trail, and revocation propagates everywhere within minutes.

### What we deploy

**Workspace topology.**
- **Internal:** AHO SOPs, runbooks, network diagrams, incident reports. Access via `grp_engineering` only.
- **Per-customer:** one top-level page per customer, gated by `grp_client_<name>` group with read access. SCIM-driven from Okta.
- **Public-link sharing:** disabled at the workspace admin level. One toggle, an entire class of accidents eliminated.

**Customer portal via Super.so + Cloudflare Tunnel + Cloudflare Access.**
The portal at `portal.tachtech.com` is a projection of underlying Notion pages, fronted by Cloudflare Tunnel (no origin IP exists for an attacker to find) and gated by Cloudflare Access (Okta-federated, customer authenticates with their corporate identity). Revoking access in Notion — driven by Okta SCIM — revokes portal access in the same step.

**CASB monitoring.** Cloudflare CASB watches Notion continuously for public-link creation events, external collaborator additions outside the customer's group, and unusual export patterns. Drift alerts within minutes. Public links are auto-revoked.

**Audit trail to long-term storage.** Notion's audit log captures every page view by every authenticated user. We export weekly to S3 with object-lock retention so the trail outlives Notion's native window. The auditor's question — *"who viewed our customer's Q3 deliverable?"* — is a query, not a project.

### Why this is non-negotiable
The alternative to a Notion-fronted, Tunnel-gated, CASB-monitored portal is sending PDFs over email. Every email-attached PDF is a permanent uncontrolled copy on someone's laptop, on a mail server, in a backup, in their Downloads folder. **We have replaced PDFs over email with revocable read access and per-view audit lines.** Same content delivered to the customer; an entirely defensible posture for us.

## Cross-Cutting: Supply Chain Hygiene

### What we defend against
The most damaging breaches of recent memory came through `npm install`, `pip install`, and container registry pulls — not through firewalls. event-stream, ua-parser-js, colors.js, xz-utils all weaponized the supply chain. Every dependency we ship is a code-signing decision delegated to a stranger; we treat it as such.

### What we deploy

**Private registry as the single source.** GitHub Packages proxies npm and container registries. Every Tachtech repo's `.npmrc` points at the proxy. Direct pulls from public registries from inside Tachtech infrastructure are blocked at L1.

**Lockfile-driven CI gate.** `npm ci` (and equivalents for other ecosystems) fails the build if lockfile and manifest disagree. CI runs `npm audit` (fail on high/critical), Sigstore provenance verification (rejects deps without signed provenance attestations), and Socket.dev typosquat scanning.

**GitHub Advanced Security.** Dependabot for high/critical CVEs with auto-PR. Secret scanning with push-protection — the commit is rejected before the secret hits the remote. CodeQL on every PR.

**Endpoint-level audit.** Kolide compares `node_modules` contents against the GitHub Packages allow-list on developer laptops. Anything unrecognized warns the user and logs the deviation.

**Image scanning at the registry.** Trivy/Grype-equivalent scans run on every container image push. Builds with unpatched critical CVEs are blocked from production tags.

**Falcon process anomaly.** Catches the runtime: a freshly installed dependency making outbound calls to a non-allowlisted destination is flagged within seconds.

### Why this is non-negotiable
When the next xz-utils happens — and it will — Tachtech needs to answer *"did we ship that?"* in minutes, not days. Our lockfiles, our provenance verification, and our endpoint audit make that answer trivial. **We do not delegate code-signing to strangers.**

## Cross-Cutting: DLP Unified Across Every Layer

DLP signatures are defined **once** in a security-team-owned repo and pushed to every enforcement plane that can apply them: Cloudflare Gateway HTTP DLP (L1), Chrome CBCM Data Protection (L4.5), Google Workspace DLP (L2), Falcon Data Protection (L4). Categories include AHO API keys, AWS access keys, GitHub fine-grained PATs, Stripe live keys, project keyword dictionaries, and per-customer identifier lists.

We test the catalog quarterly: synthesize a benign canary string in each category and confirm it triggers block-and-alert at every layer. **DLP is not theater at Tachtech — it is verifiable, every quarter, at every layer.**

## Cross-Cutting: Continuous Compliance with Drata

Drata is integrated with every preceding tool — Okta, Workspace, 1Password, Tailscale, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike, Kolide, GitHub, Notion, AWS, GCP, our HRIS, our attestation service. It continuously verifies that controls are in place. Drift triggers an alert within an hour. Personnel evidence (background checks, security training, signed AUP, offboarding completion) flows from the HRIS into the same evidence ledger.

When the SOC 2 audit window opens, the deliverable is **read-only auditor access to Drata.** The auditor pulls evidence themselves. Tachtech's audit-prep cost drops from an engineer's quarter to a coffee meeting. **The controls are real because they were real before Drata; Drata makes them provable in real time, to anyone who needs to see the proof.**

## The Attack Matrix — How the Chain Holds

This is what an attacker sees attempting to reach Tachtech customer data:

| Attack | Defeated by |
|---|---|
| Botnet probing the WAN | DMP IDS/IPS (L0) drops at the edge |
| Tor exit-node reconnaissance | DMP GeoIP / Tor block (L0) |

| Stolen Okta admin token replayed | App Connector / IP allowlist rejects source IP (L3) |
| 1Password vault server-side dump | 128-bit Secret Key + Argon2id; offline crack infeasible (L2) |
| Engineer pastes client data into Claude | Cloudflare HTTP DLP + Chrome paste DLP both block; audit log records (L1, L4.5) |
| Engineer pastes client data into ChatGPT | CBCM blocks the domain — only Claude is allowlisted (L4.5) |
| Stolen Workspace credentials + TOTP | Google APP rejects — TOTP is disallowed for the OU (L2) |
| Phished into installing a "productivity" extension | CBCM allowlist blocks install (L4.5) |
| Compromised allowlisted extension auto-update | Falcon browser sensor flags anomaly; CBCM remote-disables (L4) |
| Personal Gmail in same Chrome window | CBCM forces profile separation; cookies/storage isolated (L4.5) |
| Browser extension exfiltrating session cookies | WARP DLP + Falcon process anomaly (L1, L4) |
| AHO container resolves a DGA C2 domain | Cloudflare Gateway blocks at DNS — TCP never opens (L1) |
| Malware switches to its own DoH resolver | Gateway policy blocks DoH/DoT to non-Cloudflare (L1) |
| Compromised npm dependency beaconing C2 | WARP Gateway DNS block + Falcon process anomaly (L1, L4) |
| Container escape exploit | Falcon container sensor flags syscall pattern (L4) |
| Insider tries to upload client data to Drive | WARP DLP block + CASB alert (L1, L6) |
| Public Notion link accidentally created | CASB auto-revoke + alert (L6) |
| Stolen GitHub PAT used from new IP | GitHub IP allowlist reject (L3 / supply chain) |
| Falcon sensor process killed | Heartbeat-loss alert → Okta Universal Logout (L4 → L2) |
| Compromised host on POC1 attempts pivot to Workloads | Inter-VLAN ACL matrix denies; Tailscale ACL also denies (L3) |
| Tailscale control-plane outage | DMP backup WireGuard remains (L0) |
| Cloudflare regional outage | Unbound on subnet router serves DNS; runbook engages secondary path if needed (L1) |
| Host-root operator reads AHO container memory | SEV-SNP encryption keeps memory unreadable; attestation proves workload integrity (L5) |
| Tampered AHO container image deployed | Attestation fails; 1P Connect refuses secret release; container fails to start (L5) |
| Prompt for client-classified engagement leaked to Anthropic | Engagement-routed to on-prem inference; gateway never reaches `*.anthropic.com` (L2.5) |
| Stolen Anthropic API key | API keys are 1P Service Account scoped; CLI uses SSO; revocable instantly (L2.5) |
| AHO ingest credentials pasted into Slack DM | Workspace DLP scans Slack via CASB; redacts and alerts (L4.5) |
| Stolen Okta session replayed against Claude | Triple Lock — network and posture must also pass (L2.5) |
| Auditor asks for evidence of any control | Drata exports the evidence trail with timestamps (L∞) |

Every row is an attack class with a defined defeat at a defined layer. Every row is a control we have **deployed**, not planned. The chain holds because every link is enforced by a different system, owned by a different boundary, audited in a different log. **No single failure collapses the perimeter.**

## Six Pillars, Eleven Walls

| Pillar | Tools | The trust question we answer |
|---|---|---|
| Perimeter | Ubiquiti UDM-SE | "Did this packet come from somewhere we recognize?" |
| DNS + Egress | Cloudflare Zero Trust + Unbound fallback | "Is this query, this connection, going somewhere it should?" |

| Credentials | 1Password (vaults · CLI · SA · Connect · Watchtower) | "Where did this secret come from, when was it rotated, who used it?" |
| Transport | Tailscale (HA · 7×/26 · App Connectors) | "Can this identity reach this /26?" |
| Endpoint + Browser | Kolide · Falcon (Partner) · Chrome CBCM | "Is the device, and the browser, in a known-good state?" |
| AI | Claude Enterprise (Triple Lock) + Sovereign on-prem | "Is this AI interaction governed and routed appropriately?" |
| Workload | AHO containers + AMD SEV-SNP attestation | "Is the running code provably what we intended, encrypted in use?" |
| Knowledge | Notion + Cloudflare Tunnel + CASB | "Who has read what, and is that still appropriate?" |

| Compliance (cross-cutting) | Drata | "Can we prove all of the above, in real time, to anyone who asks?" |

**One trust decision, evaluated at every surface where data can be touched.**

We have built the security architecture our customers' compliance teams want to see and the security architecture our engineers want to ship code on top of. **These are not in tension.** The chain is fast because identity is unified, the network is segmented sensibly, and secrets materialize at runtime instead of living on disk. The chain is secure because every link defeats a class of attacks the link below it cannot see.

Our customers chose Tachtech because they have data they cannot trust to general-purpose vendors. **We do not give them a reason to question that choice.** When their compliance team reviews us, they see a chain of trust they recognize, anchored by vendors they know, verified continuously by a tool they have audited before. When the next industry-shaking breach drops on a Friday afternoon, our team reads the post-mortem and confirms — at every layer — that the failure mode does not apply to us. Not because we are lucky, but because we built the chain to assume every link will eventually be tested. **We test ours so the attacker doesn't have to.**
